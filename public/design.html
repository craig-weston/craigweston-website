01 / UNDERSTAND PEOPLE

Research before
the pixels.

User-centred design isn’t a label you stick on a slide. It’s the work of understanding people when they’re busy, distracted, confused or just trying to get on with their Tuesday.

01

Listen first.

Talk to people. Watch what actually happens. Find the workaround with three spreadsheets and a handwritten note. It usually knows more about the problem than the requirements document.

DISCOVERY / INTERVIEWS / CONTEXT
02

Connect the dots.

Turn scattered observations into journey maps, workshop themes, user flows and a shared understanding of where things go wrong. Preferably before everyone falls in love with a solution.

FRAMING / JOURNEYS / STRATEGY
03

Shape and refine.

Make it tangible. Put a prototype in somebody’s hands. Watch them do something unexpected, fix it, and repeat. That’s not a setback; it’s the useful bit.

02 / DESIGN FROM A DIFFERENT PERSPECTIVE
間 MA

Even the pause
has a purpose.

In Japanese aesthetics, ma can describe a meaningful interval in space or time. It reminds me that good design needs rhythm, not a relentless stream of information.

That influences the space I leave around important decisions, the pace of an animation and knowing when a pop-up ought to mind its own business.

03 / CULTURE MEETS CRAFT

Ideas that influence
how I design and live.

These are ideas I keep returning to, drawn from Japanese aesthetics and everyday life. I’m borrowing lessons, not claiming that a whole culture can be reduced to three useful words.

01 / SPACE

余白

よはく · YOH AKU

Space is part of the composition.

In painting, yohaku is unpainted space with compositional significance. In interfaces, I take it as permission to remove noise and give the important things room to breathe.

02 / INTERVAL

間

ま · MA

A meaningful pause.

Not every interaction needs a flourish. I want motion and timing to help people understand what just happened, without competing for attention.

03 / ENCOUNTER

一期一会

いちごいちえ · ICHIGO ICHIE

Give the present moment attention.

Drawn from Japanese tea culture, this expression encourages care for each unique encounter. It resonates with my approach to research conversations and time spent outdoors.

Sources: Japan House London on Japanese aesthetics · Omotesenke on ichi-go ichi-e · JAANUS on yohaku
